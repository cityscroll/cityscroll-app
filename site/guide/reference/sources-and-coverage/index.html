Where the records come from

Who published each record, what CityScroll adds to it, what each date means, and what a source can and cannot answer.

Last reviewed 2026-11-25

CityScroll brings together New York City notices and other public records. It also links and summarizes records so you can follow a topic across agencies. Open the source on a record to see what the publisher released and when.

Three kinds of text on a page

A page can hold three different kinds of text, and each is labeled so you can tell them apart.

What you are readingWho wrote itHow to recognize it
The public recordThe agency or other official publisherIt names the publisher and links to the published copy, for example “Source: New York City Office of Management and Budget” followed by a link to the register
A CityScroll connection or interpretationCityScrollLinks between records, plain-language summaries, and notes such as “This answer reads differently from the one published May 12, 2026.” Each is worked out from the published records beside it
Sample materialCityScroll, for practiceIt is marked as a demo or an example, such as the Demo view in WinCityWork. It is never a public record

CityScroll does not write records. A summary, a change note or a match is CityScroll's reading of the publisher's text, and the publisher's own words stay on the page next to it.

Check the source behind a record

  • Who published it. Every record names its publisher and links back to the copy they released. That link is the authority. If the two ever disagree, the publisher's copy is the one to trust.
  • Why two records are linked. A connection is a claim CityScroll makes. Open its evidence to see the identifier or the published wording it rests on, and how strong a match it is. See Check the evidence behind a connection.
  • Whether a pattern holds. A flag about the past counts earlier published records. Open the records it counted at their source before you rely on it. See Flags and historical patterns.
  • Answers from an assistant. Ask with AI lets your own assistant search CityScroll's public records. What the assistant writes is its own text, not CityScroll's and not the publisher's, so open the record it cites.

Four dates that mean different things

The dateWhat it tells youWhere you see it
Publication dateWhen the publisher released this version of the recordBeside the record, for example “Published June 30, 2026”
Record dateSomething that happens inside the record: a hearing, a deadline, an awardIn the record's own fields, such as “Response deadline”
Check dateWhen CityScroll last read the sourceIn the source line, for example “Read November 24, 2026”
Latest available recordThe newest record the publisher has released so farThe most recent publication date in a list

A recent check date with an old publication date usually means the publisher has released nothing newer, not that CityScroll is behind. For more on dates, see What dates and blanks mean.

How often the sources change

Some publishers release as things happen, and some release on a cycle. That difference is the commonest reason something the city has already done has not reached this site yet, so it is worth knowing which kind of source you are reading. This table counts only sources whose records you can reach on CityScroll. Sources used only behind the scenes are left out.

The source ledger lists every source by name, including the ones left out above. Its Status column uses four words. live means the source must keep its expected fields and stay within its freshness limit. build-time means a saved copy is built into the site and carries the date it was taken. manual means a person re-checks the publisher's page. disabled means it is not being read for the site now and stays listed so the gap is visible.

What coverage means

Coverage is not one number, and CityScroll does not offer one. It varies by source, and there are four different ways a source can be working perfectly and still not answer your question.

The caseWhat it means for what you see
The source is complete but narrowThe City Record covers what the city publishes in The City Record. Things the city does elsewhere are not in it
The source is read but not joinedThe record is here and the record that would connect it to what you are reading is not, so the connection is absent although both ends exist
The source is closedThe publisher has stopped adding to it. It stays because it is the record of its period
The source is pausedIt is not currently being read. It stays listed rather than quietly disappearing, so a stopped feed cannot be mistaken for a subject with nothing in it

A missing link is not a missing record. When no connection appears, search the publisher's own dataset before concluding that no official record exists. A blank field means the publisher left it blank. It is not a zero and not a “no”.

What is not covered

  • Anything the city has not published. Where a decision was made and no public record of it was released, you will not find it here. That absence tells you about publication and nothing about the decision.
  • Anything published in a form that cannot be joined. A document released only as a scan, or with no identifier tying it to anything else, may be published and still unreachable from a record you are reading.
  • Anything private. Records about identifiable individuals, such as exam scores or applicant details, are not published by the city as open data and are not here.

When a source could not be read, and when there is nothing

A source that could not be reached is a different thing from a source with nothing to say, and the site keeps them apart. A page that could not load its records says so. Reload to try again, or open the official source. Treat it as a fact about today's read, not about the city.

A genuinely empty answer says it is empty. Even then it describes the copies CityScroll holds, so the publisher's own dataset is the place to confirm it.

A budget request is not a contracting opportunity

This example uses the New York City Office of Management and Budget's Register of Community Board Budget Requests, as checked on November 25, 2026.

  1. Open Bronx Community Board 1 and find Budget requests this district made to city agencies. Under Choose an agency, choose Department of Education.
  2. Find request 101202708C, for fiscal year 2027. The board asked for a library and media room at PS 5, and for entrance cameras and School Safety Agents. This is the board's request.
  3. Read the answers below it. Both, published May 12, 2026 and June 30, 2026, say “Agency supports but cannot accommodate” and add that funding is not available. This is the agency's published response. CityScroll notes that the second answer reads the same as the first.
  4. Check the source line at the foot of the section. It names the publisher and the day CityScroll last read the register, and links to the published register, where tracking code 101202708C shows the same request and answers.

A request and its response are a dated signal of what a board asked for and what an agency said about it. They are not funding and not a contract. Whether the city is buying anything is a separate question, answered by a solicitation record: look at the solicitations still accepting responses on Browse contracts, or search PASSPort public solicitations. Finding nothing there means no matching solicitation is open today, not that the request was refused.
