## Your task

Find the records the city has published about a topic you care about, narrow them to the one kind of
record you actually want, and keep a link that brings you back to the same view.

## Before you start

- You need no account and no email address. Everything on this page is reading.
- Open [CityScroll search](/search/). Typing a topic into the box on the [front page](/) lands in the
  same place.
- The search page assembles its results in your browser, so it needs JavaScript switched on. The
  record pages it sends you to do not.

## Step 1 — Search for the topic, not the record type

Type an ordinary word — a street, a landlord, an agency, a job title — and press Search. The address
becomes `cityscroll.org/search/?q=` followed by your word.

Results come back grouped by the kind of civic object they are, rather than as one ranked list. The
groups are Contracts, People and organizations, Land, Rules, Meetings, and Exams. Each result also
says why it matched, and a result matched on a passage carries the sentence from the published
record under a line reading **Related because this source passage contains the topic words**.

How many results each group holds depends on what the city published recently, so the counts will
not match anyone else's. Read which groups filled up, not the totals.

> Checkpoint: you can name the groups that hold results for your word, and say what kind of record each one contains.

## Step 2 — Move to the collection that holds your kind of record

Search is for finding a starting record. Once you know the kind of record you want, work inside that
collection, where the filters for that kind live. [Browse](/browse/) lists every collection with
what it covers and what it can be filtered by.

Three of them are about people, and they are genuinely different destinations. Choosing the wrong
one is the most common way to conclude a record does not exist when it does.

- **People + organizations** — [Browse people and organizations](/browse/people/). Community Boards,
  City Council, agencies and vendors. Every row names its institution. Look up a body here — a
  board, a council, an agency, a vendor. Job records live in the other two.
- **Staffing** — [Browse appointments](/browse/staffing/). Appointments, payroll, eligible lists and
  hiring outcomes — what happened to positions.
- **Exams** — [Browse exams](/browse/exams/). Civil-service exam schedules, applications, eligible
  lists and published outcomes — how you would sit one.

> Checkpoint: the page you are on names the collection you chose, and its filters are about that kind of record.

## Step 3 — Narrow inside the collection

Each collection offers the filters that make sense for it, and only those. Contracts filters by
keyword, agency and minimum award amount. Meetings filters by keyword, date window, borough and
agency. Land filters by keyword, action type, review procedure and stage. Rules filters by keyword
and agency. Exams filters by job or exam number, eligibility, interest area and exam window.

Time and place are not offered everywhere, because they do not mean the same thing everywhere. A
meeting has a date and a borough; a registered contract has an award amount and an agency. Use what
the collection gives you rather than looking for a control it does not have.

> Checkpoint: the list shortened, and the filters you set are shown as the ones in force.

## Step 4 — Keep the narrowed view

Copy the address out of your browser's address bar. Everything you chose is in it, so opening it
later — or sending it to someone else — rebuilds the same narrowed view against whatever the city
has published by then.

That link is worth more than a screenshot, because it stays current. It carries nothing about you.

> Checkpoint: pasting the address into a new tab reopens the same collection with the same filters set.

## Step 5 — Read one record to the end

Open a single record and read what it actually says. A civil-service exam is a good example, because
it shows the whole shape at once: [Caseworker, exam 7016](/exams/7016/) has an application window of
07/01/2026 to 07/21/2026, and that window is marked **closed**.

A closed window is not a broken page. The record stays complete and useful — the fee, the salary,
the qualifications, the official notice, and what usually happens next — and CityScroll says the
window has passed instead of inviting you to apply to something you cannot apply to. Every record
carries a link to the city's own published copy, and that copy is the one to trust.

> Checkpoint: you can say what the record is, what state it is in today, and where the city published it.

## When nothing comes back

An empty result is a real answer, and it usually means one of four things. Work through them in this
order rather than retyping the same search.

- **Your filters are too narrow.** The collection tells you so and offers a way back out — Land, for
  example, says the current filters match nothing and offers **Clear filters, show all**. Widen one
  filter at a time so you learn which one was doing it.
- **You are in the wrong collection.** Go back to the topic search and look at which groups filled
  up. A word that finds nothing under Exams may find plenty under Staffing.
- **The word is not the city's word.** Published records use the agency's vocabulary. Try the formal
  name, the address, the agency, or the exam number.
- **The city has not published anything.** That is a finding, not a failure, and the honest next
  step is to check the [City Record Online](https://a856-cityrecord.nyc.gov/) directly rather than
  assume a record exists.

If a page tells you a source could not be reached, that is different again — it means the answer is
unknown right now, not that there is nothing. Try it again later before drawing a conclusion.

## You are done when

You are on one collection, narrowed by filters you chose, with a record open that you can read to
the end and check against the city's own copy — and the address in your browser reopens exactly that
view tomorrow.
