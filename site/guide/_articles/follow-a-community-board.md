## Your task

Follow one community board — for this example, Manhattan Community Board 7 — and understand what
the resulting updates do and do not cover.

## Before you start

- Open [Following](/following/).
- Know your board as a **pair**: a borough and a number. A board number on its own does not identify
  a board anywhere in New York City.
- Read [Follow a search and manage your updates](/guide/how-to/follow-a-search/) first if you have
  not created a watch before. This page only covers the part that is specific to boards.

## A board number is not a district number

New York City has two numbered geographies that both get called "district seven", and they are not
the same thing.

- **Community Boards** are numbered 1 to 18 **within each borough**. There is a Manhattan Community
  Board 7, a Brooklyn Community Board 7, and so on. Only the pair means anything.
- **City Council Districts** are numbered 1 to 51 **across the whole city**. There is exactly one
  City Council District 7, and it is not connected to any board's number.

CityScroll keeps them as two separate choices rather than one numbered box. A community board is
picked under the **Hearings and meetings** topic; council-district updates are their own topic,
**City Council District weekly**, listed under **More topics**. That topic's district field carries
a note reading "Not a Community Board", the numbering of each, and a link straight to the board
picker. If you find yourself typing 7 into a box labelled 1 to 51, you are on the wrong control.

## Step 1 — Choose the hearings and meetings topic

Under **What do you want to follow?**, choose **Hearings and meetings**. The board picker belongs to
that topic, so it appears once the topic is chosen and not before.

> Checkpoint: Hearings and meetings is the selected topic, and a Community Board group is visible under Narrow it down.

## Step 2 — Pick the borough, then the board

Open **Narrow it down**. Inside it is a **Community Board** group with two dropdowns.

1. Choose the borough — Manhattan.
2. Choose the board number — 7.

The help line under them says the choice covers that board's meetings. Both dropdowns must be set:
until the pair is complete, there is no board to watch.

> Checkpoint: the two dropdowns read Manhattan and 7, and the board is now named in full rather than as a number.

## Step 3 — Preview, and read the rule sentence

Press **Preview matches**.

Two things now name the board in full. The **Watch summary** rule sentence reads "Notify me when
meetings for Manhattan Community Board 7 are published", and the **Watch criteria** panel below it
carries a **Community Board** entry reading Manhattan Community Board 7.

The full name is the thing to check. Seeing "Manhattan Community Board 7" rather than "7" is how you
know the borough was carried through and you are not about to follow a board in another borough.

> Checkpoint: the criteria and the rule sentence both say Manhattan Community Board 7 in full.

## Step 4 — Look at what the matches actually cover

Follow **See current matches** from the watch summary. It opens the map view for **Manhattan Community District 7**,
with meetings shown, rather than a list of that board's own agendas.

That is the honest shape of this watch, and it is worth understanding before you rely on it. The
board's own name is preserved everywhere you are shown it, but the records it gathers are matched by
**the community district the board covers** — a place — not by the question of who called the
meeting. Two consequences follow.

- **You will see more than the board's own meetings.** A meeting another body holds about something
  in that district can match. That is usually what a resident wants, and it is not a claim that the
  board convened it.
- **Do not read a match as a board proceeding.** Each record names the body that is actually holding
  it. Read that line before you describe a meeting as your board's.

If you want the board's own committees, membership and published record instead, the board's page
under [Community Boards](/community-boards/) is the place for that, and it is a different question
from being emailed about a place.

> Checkpoint: you can look at one match and say which body is holding it, without assuming.

## Step 5 — Create the watch

Choose **Daily** or **Weekly** under Email frequency, enter your email address under **Create this
watch**, and press **Create watch**. The watch exists from that moment; a welcome email follows with
the link you will use to change or stop it later.

## When something is missing

- **The picker is not there.** The chosen topic is not Hearings and meetings. Change the topic and
  the picker appears.
- **The preview is empty.** Boards meet on a schedule, and a district can be quiet for weeks. The
  preview says so plainly, and a watch on a quiet district still works — it exists to tell you about
  meetings that have not been published yet.
- **The board's own recent meetings are not all there.** CityScroll shows what has been published in
  the sources it reads, and it marks a source it could not check rather than reporting an empty list
  as a fact. Where a board publishes its own calendar, that remains the authoritative one.

## You are done when

You have a watch whose criteria and rule sentence both name your board in full, borough included,
and you can say — before any email arrives — that its matches are the meetings tied to that board's
district rather than only the meetings the board itself convenes.
